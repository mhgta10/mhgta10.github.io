# About Me

I am a PhD researcher in Marine Science at Tongji University, China. My research lies at the intersection of marine geochemistry, marine geology, and paleoceanography, with a particular focus on the interactions between tectonics, continental chemical weathering, and the global carbon cycle.

My work aims to understand how geological processes regulate atmospheric CO₂ concentrations and influence Earth's climate over geological timescales. By integrating geochemical proxies, sedimentary records, and tectonic reconstructions, I investigate the mechanisms linking Earth's interior, surface processes, and climate evolution.

## Research Interests

- Continental chemical weathering
- Global carbon cycle dynamics
- Marine sediment geochemistry
- Tectonic-climate interactions
- Paleoceanography
- Earth system evolution

## Current Research

My current research focuses on the geological controls of continental chemical weathering and their implications for long-term carbon cycling. In particular, I am interested in how tectonic activity, lithological composition, and weathering intensity influence atmospheric CO₂ consumption and climate regulation through Earth's history.
