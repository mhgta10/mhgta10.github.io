### Hi, I'm Muhammad Habibullah Galih Tri Aji

I am a PhD researcher with a strong interest in <span class="blue-bold">marine geochemistry</span>, <span class="blue-bold">marine geology</span>, and <span class="blue-bold">paleoceanography</span>. My research focuses on understanding how tectonic processes, continental weathering, and the global carbon cycle interact to influence **Earth's climate over geological timescales**.

My work seeks to unravel the links between <span class="blue-bold">tectonic activity</span>, <span class="blue-bold">chemical weathering</span>, and <span class="blue-bold">carbon cycling</span>, with particular emphasis on how these processes regulate atmospheric CO₂ concentrations and contribute to long-term climate evolution. By integrating geological observations, geochemical proxies, and marine sediment records, I aim to better understand the mechanisms that have controlled Earth's climate throughout Earth's history.

Originally from Indonesia, I have always been fascinated by the natural processes that shape our planet. This curiosity led me to pursue research in Earth sciences, where I can combine field observations, laboratory analyses, and geochemical data to investigate questions related to climate change and Earth system evolution.

My current research interests include <span class="blue-bold">continental chemical weathering</span>, <span class="blue-bold">global carbon cycle dynamics</span>, <span class="blue-bold">marine sediment geochemistry</span>, <span class="blue-bold">tectonic–climate interactions</span>, <span class="blue-bold">paleoceanography</span>, and broader questions related to <span class="blue-bold">Earth system evolution</span>. I am particularly interested in understanding how mountain building, lithological variability, and weathering processes influence carbon sequestration and environmental change from regional to global scales.

Beyond research, I enjoy sharing scientific knowledge, mentoring students, and engaging in discussions about Earth history, climate, and environmental change. Outside academia, I have a strong passion for <span class="blue-bold">Bouldering</span>, and <span class="blue-bold">Taekwondo</span>, which has taught me discipline, perseverance, and the importance of continuous self-improvement—values that I also apply to my scientific work.

Through interdisciplinary research that combines geochemistry, sedimentology, and Earth system science, I seek to contribute to a better understanding of the processes that govern **long-term climate regulation** and the co-evolution of Earth's surface environments.
